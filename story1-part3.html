Sensual Encounters

Deep Romance · The Storm

The storm rolled in faster than the forecast had promised, turning the sky the color of a fresh bruise by six o'clock. Elena had meant to leave that morning — had even packed her bag and set it by the door — but one excuse became another, and now the ferry was cancelled and the roads were reportedly flooding past the second bridge, and she was, undeniably, stuck.

"You could pretend to be more upset about this," Daniel said, lighting the row of candles they kept for exactly this kind of coastal emergency, the power having flickered out twenty minutes earlier. "I'm devastated," Elena said flatly, and he laughed — a real laugh, the kind she remembered from before either of them had learned to be careful with each other — and something in her chest loosened that had been tight for the better part of ten years.

They ate crackers and the last of the good cheese by candlelight, wind throwing rain against the windows in long percussive sheets, and talked about nothing that mattered and everything that did: her almost-marriage, his almost-different-life, the specific loneliness of building a career instead of a home. "I used to think I left because of what happened with us," Daniel said eventually, staring into his glass. "I don't think that's true anymore. I think I left because staying meant admitting how much I needed you to still want me."

The confession sat between them, unguarded in a way neither of them had managed in the decade since. Elena reached across the small table, not entirely sure of her own intentions until her hand was already covering his. "I never stopped," she said, and the words cost her more than any letter in that shoebox ever had. Outside, the storm did not care about any of it, and inside, for the first time in ten years, neither did they.

Later, with the candles burned low and the rain gentling into something almost peaceful, they sat shoulder to shoulder on the porch daybed, not talking, not needing to. Elena thought about the bag still packed by the front door and found, for the first time since arriving, that she was in no particular hurry to use it.
